Organoid intelligence · Responsible AI

When donated cells become part of a computer

By Dr. Salman Ahmed Khatani · Karachi, Pakistan · 19 November 2026

What should a cell donor be told if their cells could one day be used in a computer?

In the Brainoware experiment, researchers used the electrical responses of a brain organoid, a three-dimensional cluster of living neural tissue, as part of a hybrid computing system. One task was distinguishing speakers from audio recordings. The experiment demonstrated a specific pattern-recognition capability. Whether such a system could have a subjective experience remains an open question.

What did the donor agree to?

Imagine a donor in Karachi agrees to provide cells for research into a disease that runs in their family. Years later, a company proposes using tissue derived from those cells for commercial computing. The researcher may see continuity between the projects. The donor may see two quite different promises. Who gets to decide when a change in purpose is significant enough to ask again?

A question for South Asian classrooms

Invite students to take the roles of donor, researcher, ethics committee member and entrepreneur. Ask each to write one sentence explaining the proposed new use to the donor. Compare which sentences describe the commercial purpose, acknowledge uncertainty and give the donor a meaningful choice. Then try the same conversation in the language the donor speaks at home. Which details become clearer? Which are hardest to explain?

Agar aap ke diye hue cells ko baad mein computer ka hissa banaya jaye, toh aap pehle kya jaanna chahenge?
