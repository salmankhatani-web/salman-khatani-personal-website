# When donated cells become part of a computer

**Dr. Salman Ahmed Khatani** · Karachi, Pakistan · Foresight teaching note

What should a cell donor be told if their cells could one day be used in a computer?

In the Brainoware experiment, researchers used the electrical responses of a brain organoid as part of a hybrid computing system. One task was distinguishing speakers in a collection of audio recordings. The result demonstrates a specific pattern-recognition capability. Whether a system has subjective experience is a separate, open question.

## A question for South Asian classrooms

Imagine a donor in Karachi consents to the use of their cells for disease research. Years later, a company proposes using tissue derived from those cells for commercial computing. What did the original agreement mean to the donor? What new decision should they be able to make?

Ask students to take the roles of donor, researcher, ethics committee member and entrepreneur. Each writes one sentence explaining the proposed new use to the donor. Compare who describes the commercial purpose, who explains uncertainty, and who gives the donor a meaningful choice. For a bilingual class, try explaining the same choice in English and in the language the donor uses at home.

*Roman Urdu discussion prompt:* Agar aap ke diye hue cells ko baad mein computer ka hissa banaya jaye, toh aap pehle kya jaanna chahenge?
